Mount Everest 29er*

Mount Everest is Earth's highest mountain above sea level at 29,032 ft — the crown jewel of human mountaineering. But it makes this list with an asterisk: measured base-to-summit, the way every other peak here is, Everest rises only ~12,000–15,000 ft from its base on the Tibetan Plateau and wouldn't qualify as a 29er at all. It is here on sea-level fame alone — out-topped from base to peak by two ocean-rooted Hawaiian volcanoes, and dwarfed by a tilted block of crust on a moon of Jupiter.

First climbed in 1953 by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay, it remains the ultimate Earth-bound summit and a humbling reminder of how modest our tallest mountain really is. Its nearest sea, the Bay of Bengal, is some 700 km away.

* Mount Everest is listed by its 29,032 ft elevation above sea level — the number that made it famous as Earth’s highest peak. Measured base-to-summit like every other mountain here, Everest rises only ~12,000–15,000 ft from its base on the Tibetan Plateau and wouldn’t qualify as a 29er at all. Earth’s real base-to-peak giants are the Hawaiian volcanoes Mauna Kea (33,500 ft) and Mauna Loa (30,085 ft). The world’s most famous mountain makes this list on a technicality.

Quick facts
  • Listed by its 29,032 ft elevation above sea level — not base-to-peak, so it wears an asterisk here
  • Its true base-to-summit relief is only ~12,000–15,000 ft, short of the 29,000 ft bar
  • First summited in 1953 by Hillary and Tenzing Norgay
  • Out-topped base-to-peak by both Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa
  • Even Mars's third-string volcanoes look down on it

Routes

  • South Col (Nepal) — the classic line
  • North Col / Northeast Ridge (Tibet)

Real, hard, and deadly — thousands have summited since 1953 and hundreds have died trying. But on this list it is the gentle one: the only peak with breathable air, and the shortest of the bunch.

Trip Reports

Everest has more trip reports than any mountain on Earth — just not here. On 29ers.com it is simply the shortest peak on the list. Humbling, isn’t it?

Conditions — Mount Everest

Actual weather! Wind, snow, and breathable air (less of it the higher you go). Comparatively hospitable — and, by solar-system standards, comparatively tiny. Compared with every other peak on this list, Everest has weather you could (barely) survive.
