Mauna Kea 29er

Mauna Kea is a dormant Hawaiian shield volcano whose summit reaches 13,803 ft above sea level — but whose base lies deep on the Pacific seafloor. Measured base-to-peak it rises about 10.2 km (33,500 ft), making it the tallest mountain on Earth by total height, even though only its top third pokes above the waves.

Its dry, high, stable summit hosts one of the world's premier collections of astronomical observatories. Mauna Kea last erupted roughly 4,500–6,000 years ago and holds Lake Waiau, among the highest lakes in the Pacific basin.

Quick facts
  • Summit is 13,803 ft above sea level but ~33,500 ft from its seafloor base
  • Tallest mountain on Earth measured base-to-peak
  • Last erupted roughly 4,500–6,000 years ago
  • Hosts 13 observatories funded by 11 countries
  • Earth's true champion — if you count the underwater part

Routes

  • Summit Road — drive to ~13,800 ft
  • Humuʻula Trail — ~6 mi on foot

The catch: only the top ~13,800 ft is above water. The other ~19,700 ft of this mountain is underwater, so nobody actually climbs the full ~33,500 ft. Still counts.

Conditions — Mauna Kea

Actual weather! Wind, snow, and breathable air (less of it the higher you go). Comparatively hospitable — and, by solar-system standards, comparatively tiny. The only peak here you can summit by road — bring a jacket, not a spacesuit.

  Summit: 33,500 ft (~10.2 km from the Pacific seafloor).
