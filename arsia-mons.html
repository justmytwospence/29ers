Arsia Mons 29er

Arsia Mons is the southernmost of the three great Tharsis Montes shield volcanoes on Mars, rising about 11.7 km above the datum and more than 9 km above the surrounding plains. It is enormous — roughly 435 km wide with a summit caldera over 100 km across — and is the second-largest volcano known by volume after Olympus Mons.

It is famous for a recurring elongated orographic cloud that forms over its summit during southern winter, and it hosts several putative cave-entrance pits nicknamed “Dena,” “Chloe,” and “Wendy.”

Quick facts
  • About 435 km across with a 110–138 km summit caldera
  • Roughly 30 times the volume of Hawaii's Mauna Loa
  • Second-largest known volcano by volume after Olympus Mons
  • Hosts a recurring orographic cloud each southern winter
  • Contains several possible cave-entrance pits

Routes (1)

  • Standard Route — Class 6 (Mars environment; spacecraft & pressure suit required)

The standard ascent of this shield volcano gains roughly 38,400 ft of vertical relief. There are no technical cruxes — the entire difficulty is logistics, cold, and a hard vacuum (or near-vacuum).

Conditions — Arsia Mons

Thin carbon-dioxide air (about 600 Pa at the datum, far less at these summits), daytime highs near −20°F that plunge below −150°F at night, and planet-girdling dust storms. A pressure suit is mandatory.

  Summit: 38,400 ft (~11.7 km above the datum (>9 km above the plains)).
